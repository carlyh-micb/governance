# ToIP Approved Deliverable Process Timeline - DRAFT
[Confluence source](https://lf-toip.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/HOME/pages/767787009/ToIP+Approved+Deliverable+Process)

The goal of this process is to enable and facilitate timely review of a request from a ToIP Working Group (WG) to the ToIP Steering Committee (SC) to approve a WG Approved Deliverable as a ToIP Approved Deliverable.

## Initiation

The WG may request either a **standard review** (recommended) or an **expedited review**. In either case, the process begins as follows:

1. The WG MUST follow the Deliverable Process to complete and approve a Working Group Approved Deliverable.

2. The WG MUST complete the **Request for Initiation of Approved Deliverable Process** form and notify the Steering Committee of its completion.

3. The WG MUST prepare the **Review Package**.

4. The WG MUST submit the Review Package to the SC Chairs and request either:
   - Standard Review, or
   - Expedited Review.

## Standard Review

The standard review process takes at least **30 days**, equivalent to approximately two Steering Committee meeting cycles. The process may take **60 days or longer** depending on the number of review cycles required.

1. At the first SC meeting following receipt of the Review Package, the SC MUST appoint a Review Committee.

2. The Review Committee SHALL proceed with the Review Process.

3. If the Review Process can be completed within one SC meeting cycle, the Review Committee SHALL submit its Review Committee Report to the SC at least three business days before the next SC meeting.

4. If the Review Committee Report recommends approval and the SC is satisfied with the report:

   1. The SC SHALL proceed with a vote to approve.

5. If the Review Committee Report recommends approval but the SC is not satisfied with the report:

   1. The SC SHALL provide instructions to the Review Committee regarding required remediation.
   2. The process repeats from Step 2.

6. If the Review Committee Report does not recommend approval:

   1. The SC SHALL discuss required remediations with the Review Committee.
   2. The Review Committee SHALL communicate those remediations to the WG.
   3. After the WG completes the remediations, the process repeats from Step 2.
  
## Expedited Review

The expedited review process follows the same process as the Standard Review process, except that special Steering Committee meetings or email ballots may be used to accelerate the review timeline.

1. The WG MAY request one or more special meetings of the Steering Committee to:

   - Appoint the Review Committee.
   - Review the Review Committee Report.
   - Hold the final approval vote.

2. These special meetings MAY be conducted through email ballots.

> Because a special Steering Committee meeting, whether conducted in person or through an email ballot, requires at least one week's notice, and because the review process requires at least two Steering Committee meetings, the minimum duration of the expedited review process is two weeks.

## Public Review

1. Prior to final Working Group approval of a deliverable, the WG MUST conduct a Public Review of at least 30 calendar days in accordance with the Deliverable Process.

2. All comments received during the Public Review process MUST be reviewed and resolved to the satisfaction of the WG.

3. If substantial changes are made to the deliverable as a result of the Public Review process, the deliverable MUST undergo an additional Public Review period of at least 15 calendar days.

## Request for Initiation of Approved Deliverable Process

The WG SHALL make an official request to begin the ToIP Approved Deliverable Process by submitting the Request for Review package to the Steering Committee Co-Chairs using the designated request form.

### Required Submission Materials

The Review Package MUST include:

1. A link to the WG Approved Deliverable on GitHub, including both HTML and Markdown versions where applicable.

2. A summary of major changes since the previous version, if applicable.

3. A summary report of feedback received during Public Review and a description of how that feedback was resolved.

4. An indication of whether Standard Review or Expedited Review is being requested, including any relevant deadlines or scheduling considerations (for example, industry conferences or publication timelines).

5. Proposed members of the Review Committee, including any recommended external experts.

   > A recommended committee consists of at least three members:
   >
   > - One Steering Committee member.
   > - One individual closely involved with the work.
   > - One individual not directly involved with the work but possessing relevant expertise or familiarity with the subject matter.

## Review Package

The WG SHALL make an official request for approval by submitting the Request for Review package to the Steering Committee Co-Chairs using the designated review request process.

### Review Package Contents

The Review Package MUST include:

1. A Working Group prepared draft of the Review Committee Report.

2. A completed draft of the Review Committee Checklist for each deliverable included in the submission.

> The more complete these draft documents are, the less work is required from the Review Committee and the faster the review process can proceed.

## Review Committee

The Steering Committee SHALL establish a Review Committee to evaluate the deliverable and provide a recommendation regarding approval.

### Membership Requirements

1. The Review Committee MUST be chaired by a member of the Steering Committee.

   The Chair is responsible for overseeing preparation of the Review Committee Report and serving as the primary liaison with the Steering Committee.

2. The Review Committee MUST consist of at least three members, including the Chair.

3. Membership MUST be drawn from both:

   - ToIP members, and
   - Invited experts.

4. Membership selection MUST follow these guidelines:

   1. Participation from ToIP and LF Decentralized Trust members SHOULD be solicited through an announcement of the Review Committee.

   2. Membership MUST include invited experts with relevant expertise who were not directly involved in the development of the deliverable.

   3. Invited experts SHOULD collectively provide expertise across:

      - The problem and requirements domain.
      - The solution and implementation domain.

### Status Reporting

The Review Committee MUST keep the Steering Committee informed of progress through:

- Steering Committee mailing list updates;
- Steering Committee Discord discussions; and/or
- Reports provided during Steering Committee meetings.

## Review Process

The Review Committee SHALL conduct the review process in a manner it considers efficient and effective while meeting the requested review timeline.

### Review Activities

1. The Review Committee MUST self-organize and establish whatever review procedures it considers necessary to produce a complete Review Committee Report within the required timeframe.

2. The Review Committee MUST request review and input from invited experts on the portions of the deliverable most relevant to their expertise.

3. The primary output of the review process MUST be a completed Review Committee Report, including all required Review Committee Checklists.

4. If multiple deliverables with related or overlapping scope are submitted simultaneously:

   - The Review Committee MAY consolidate reviews into a single Review Committee Report.
   - A separate Review Committee Checklist MUST be completed for each deliverable.
   - There MUST always be one checklist per deliverable, regardless of how the report itself is organized.
  
## Review Committee Report

The Review Committee Report MUST be completed using the designated Review Committee Report template and MUST include the following sections, even if a section consists of only a single sentence.

### Required Sections

1. **Executive Summary**

   A summary of the Review Committee's findings and overall recommendation.

2. **Review Committee Members**

   A list of all Review Committee participants.

3. **Deliverable Description**

   A brief description of each WG Approved Deliverable submitted for review.

   > Multiple deliverables may be reviewed together and included in a single Review Committee Report.

4. **Deliverable Links**

   Links to each WG Approved Deliverable under review, including both HTML and Markdown versions where applicable.

5. **Reserved DOI**

   The reserved DOI associated with the specification, generated according to the DOI reservation process.

6. **Overall Assessment / Readiness**

   An overall assessment of the deliverable's readiness for approval.

7. **Readiness Determination**

   The Review Committee MUST select one of the following:

   - Ready for ToIP approval without issue.
   - Ready for ToIP approval contingent upon resolution of identified issues.
   - Not ready for ToIP approval.
   - Other.

8. **Critical Issues (MUST FIX)**

   Issues that must be resolved before approval can be recommended.

9. **Important Issues (SHOULD FIX)**

   Issues that should be resolved prior to approval unless justification is provided.

10. **Minor or Editorial Issues (MAY FIX)**

    Non-blocking issues or editorial recommendations.

11. **Security, Privacy, and Interoperability Notes**

    Any findings related to security, privacy, interoperability, implementation concerns, or deployment risks.

12. **Final Recommendation**

    The Review Committee's recommendation regarding approval.

13. **Associated Review Committee Checklists**

    A list of all Review Committee Checklists completed as part of the review.

14. **Appendices**

    Any supporting material, including:

    - Reviewer comments
    - Meeting notes
    - Issue summaries
    - Change logs
    - Other supporting documentation

## Review Committee Checklist

A Review Committee Checklist MUST be completed and submitted with the Review Committee Report.

A separate checklist MUST be completed for each deliverable under review.

The checklist SHOULD use the designated Review Committee Checklist template.

# Document Readiness

## Content Completeness

- [ ] All sections fully completed (no TODOs, placeholders, or “TBD”).
- [ ] All diagrams, examples, tables, and references included and final.
- [ ] All normative statements use IETF RFC 2119 keywords (MUST/SHOULD/MAY).
- [ ] If the deliverable is a specification, all sections (except the introduction and boilerplate sections) are marked as Normative or Informative.
- [ ] All references are included and properly classified as Normative and Informative.
- [ ] Security and Privacy Consideration sections are finalized and complete.
- [ ] Glossary and definitions are complete and consistent and reference the ToIP Glossary family whenever possible.

## Internal Consistency

- [ ] Terminology is consistent across the spec.
- [ ] There are no contradictions between sections.
- [ ] All references to numbered items (e.g., sections, algorithms, figures) are correct.
- [ ] All examples match the normative definitions and schemas.

## Technical Quality

### Normative Content

- [ ] Requirements are testable and unambiguous.
- [ ] All algorithms or flows have clearly defined inputs, outputs, and error behavior.
- [ ] Schemas validate correctly (if machine-readable schemas are included).

### Interoperability & Implementability

- [ ] Mandatory-to-implement features are clear.
- [ ] Optional features are clearly labeled, with rules for negotiation or detection.
- [ ] Versioning, extensibility, and backwards-compatibility rules are explicit.
- [ ] Conformance criteria align with test suite requirements.

## Editorial Quality

### Style & Formatting

- [ ] The deliverable follows the ToIP style guide.
- [ ] All abbreviations are defined in the Terminology section and at first use.
- [ ] Typography, heading structure, and captioning are consistent.
- [ ] The document uses valid HTML or Markdown (depending on publishing platform).

### Clarity & Readability

- [ ] Long paragraphs broken into logical chunks.
- [ ] Complex concepts explained with examples.
- [ ] Diagrams improve comprehension and match the text.

## Metadata & Ancillary Materials

### Document Metadata

- [ ] Title, version number, date, are correct.
- [ ] The status of the document is a Working Group Approved Deliverable.
- [ ] The editors and contributors are listed with proper attribution.
- [ ] Change history / release notes are current.
- [ ] The author has logged into Zenodo (free), reserved a DOI and inserted the DOI into the specification.
- [ ] The Zenodo version metadata is complete and matches the specification (e.g. authors listed in the specification are listed as authors in the Zenodo record).

### Links and References

- [ ] All external references include stable links (ideally permalinks).
- [ ] All normative references are final versions (not drafts).
- [ ] Informative references are clearly marked.

## Publishing Readiness

### IPR and Licensing

- [ ] Specification licensing text is included and correct.
- [ ] All contributors acknowledged as per Working Group policy.
- [ ] Any patent disclosures are filed (if required under the JDF process and WG patent licensing policy).
- [ ] There are no licensing conflicts with referenced content.

### Artifacts

- [ ] Supporting files (schemas, JSON-LD contexts, vocabularies, examples, test artifacts) are published and linked.
- [ ] Any accompanying test suite is current and aligned with the normative statements in the specification.
- [ ] Any implementation or interoperability reports (if required) are complete.

### Final Checks

- [ ] Spellcheck and grammar are clean.
- [ ] The document passes broken link checks.
- [ ] The final PDF/HTML rendered versions have been reviewed manually.
- [ ] The publishing pipeline or repo build passes cleanly.
# Specification Publication

Following approval by the ToIP Steering Committee, an approved specification may be versioned and published using persistent identifiers and publication records to support long-term citation and discoverability.

## Persistent DOI Link

The Digital Object Identifier (DOI) system provides persistent, globally unique, and resolvable identifiers for digital publications.

A DOI enables a specification to be:

- Uniquely identified.
- Permanently referenced.
- Reliably cited over time.
- Located even if publication URLs change.

The DOI should be considered the canonical citation reference for a published specification.

### DOI Reservation

ToIP specifications use the Zenodo repository service to reserve and publish DOIs.

Specification authors should:

1. Create a Zenodo account.

2. Create a new Zenodo upload.

3. [Reserve a DOI for the specification](https://info.figshare.com/user-guide/how-to-reserve-a-doi/).

4. Insert the reserved DOI into the specification prior to publication.

Including the DOI in the published specification ensures that the official released version contains the citation identifier needed for long-term referencing.

### Publishing to Zenodo

After the reserved DOI has been added to the specification:

1. Publish the released specification in its official form.

2. Upload the publication artifacts to Zenodo as appropriate, including:

   - Release archives generated by GitHub.
   - PDF versions.
   - Additional publication files.

3. Complete all required Zenodo metadata fields.

4. Ensure that the metadata accurately reflects the published specification.

Recommended metadata includes:

- Title (including the version in the title e.g. v1.0.0)
- Authors
- Description
- Keywords
- Related links

If a web-rendered version of the specification exists, it is recommended that a link to that rendered version be included in the Zenodo description.

### Trust over IP Zenodo Community

Trust over IP maintains a Zenodo Community that aggregates official ToIP publications.

After publishing a specification:

1. Submit the publication to the Trust over IP Zenodo Community.

2. Notify the Steering Committee that:
   - The publication has been completed.
   - The community submission request has been made.

3. The Steering Committee or designated reviewers may then approve inclusion in the Trust over IP Zenodo Community.

## Publication Deliverables Checklist

- [ ] DOI reserved.

- [ ] DOI inserted into the specification.

- [ ] Release version created.

- [ ] Publication artifacts uploaded to Zenodo.

- [ ] Zenodo metadata completed.

- [ ] Version information is included in the title and verified.

- [ ] Author information verified.

- [ ] Rendered specification URL included in metadata (if applicable).

- [ ] Publication submitted to the Trust over IP Zenodo Community.

- [ ] Steering Committee notified of publication.

- [ ] Community submission accepted.

- [ ] Publication record verified after release.
